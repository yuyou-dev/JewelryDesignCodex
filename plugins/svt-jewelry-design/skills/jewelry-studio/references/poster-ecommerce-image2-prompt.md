# Poster And Ecommerce Creative Reference

Use this reference only when a jewelry request needs poster or ecommerce art direction: a campaign
visual, ecommerce hero, lookbook page, PDP header, magazine page, product collage, or social cover.
It defines the visual brief only. Product truth always comes before layout style.

## On-Demand Loading

Before writing the final brief, read `poster-template-knowledge-base.md` and apply its Template
Selection process. Treat that library as design knowledge: choose by user intent, product type,
ratio, allowed model/ecommerce/magazine/collage modules, and visual goal, rather than by keyword
replacement.

Read `high-jewelry-poster-campaign.md` only when the user explicitly asks for a complex campaign
with multiple source designs or multiple visual slots. Do not load it for an ordinary single poster.
When it is needed, use only its visual slot vocabulary and differentiation ideas; ignore all
non-visual fields.

## Core Visual Rule

Poster and ecommerce visuals present existing or accepted jewelry. They do not redesign it. Every
brief must state:

- output shape: one independent poster, ecommerce hero, PDP header, catalog page, or social cover
- selected template card, selection reason, and required visible layout modules
- reference roles: layout reference, jewelry truth reference, natural or mood reference, and prior
  accepted render when present
- product truth: category, silhouette, proportions, stone count and positions, metal and gem color,
  setting logic, structural details, and wearing structure
- composition: hero area, model detail, product cutout, product card, art scene, text block,
  navigation, CTA, labels, numbering, or caption as appropriate
- typography: exact supplied copy, minimal-copy policy, scale, placement, and forbidden text
- image character: aspect ratio, crop, lighting, background, shadows, reflections, and skin texture
  when a model appears
- constraints: no product redesign, invented claims, hidden jewelry, wrong anatomy, or text clutter

## Template Selection

Select one card from `poster-template-knowledge-base.md` for each brief.

- Understand the request and product constraints first.
- Choose a named card when the intent maps clearly to one of the six visual modes below.
- Use `default advanced jewelry poster` when no named card clearly fits.
- Include `Selected template card`, `Template selection reason`, `Required layout modules`, and
  `anti-still-life-degeneration` in every brief.
- A generic luxury still-life label is not enough. Expand it through
  `default advanced jewelry poster` and specify at least two visible poster-layout modules beyond
  the jewelry object.
- For a broad request such as “几张高级海报”, choose complementary cards instead of repeating the
  same centered still-life with minor prop changes.

## Reference Roles

- **Layout reference:** learn only composition, negative space, hierarchy, crop logic, panel
  structure, typography scale, and display rhythm. Do not copy its model identity, jewelry, brand
  text, logo, watermark, or protected product identity.
- **Jewelry truth reference:** defines category, geometry, stone count, metal and gem color, setting,
  proportions, links, decorative details, connectors, and left/right pair logic.
- **Natural or mood reference:** contributes palette, light, material mood, organic shape, sky
  feeling, paper texture, atmosphere, or collage vocabulary only.
- **Prior accepted render:** preserves the accepted jewelry identity and may supply the product view
  for a new layout or model-wearing composition.

When several references are supplied, label their roles explicitly. Layout and mood references must
never override the jewelry truth reference.

## Product Truth

Repeat the product truth inside every independent brief:

- Preserve category, silhouette, long/short proportions, stone shapes, setting positions, chain
  thickness, pendant scale, decorative counts, gemstone count, and metal direction.
- Product cutouts, model wearing, product cards, and artistic scenes must depict the same product or
  clearly stated SKU family.
- Allowed visual changes are crop, natural lighting adaptation, contact shadow, small rotation,
  cutout cleanup, panel color, layout rhythm, and a physically plausible wearing pose.
- Do not add or remove stones, chains, flowers, leaves, pearls, clasps, hinges, or connectors; do not
  change the main-stone shape, mirror direction, category, metal color, or supplied copy.
- Avoid melted structures, stretched products, hidden connection logic, fake marks, invented
  certification, or invented price.
- When sapphire or turquoise is central, state the material behavior: sapphire is deep royal-blue
  corundum with gemstone fire; turquoise is opaque polished mineral, cabochon, carving, or inlay,
  not transparent glass or generic blue-green material.

## Text Accuracy

- If the user supplies exact text, reproduce it exactly and add no extra words.
- If no text is supplied, keep copy minimal: a short title, category label, or neutral CTA only when
  the selected mode needs it.
- A Japanese magazine page may use restrained numbering, captions, and editorial details, provided
  they do not obscure the jewelry.
- Do not invent brand names, prices, websites, QR codes, social handles, watermarks, source marks,
  certifications, meaningless letters, misspellings, or extra logos.

## Wearing Mapping

- **Necklace or pendant:** show neck, collarbone, and pendant area; keep hair and clothing away from
  chain and pendant details.
- **Earrings:** show ear, side face, jawline, and neck edge; hair must not cover the earring.
- **Ring:** show a natural hand and correct fingers; the ring must sit at a physically credible scale.
- **Bracelet or bangle:** show wrist and cuff area; preserve clasp, hinge, opening, and articulation.

The model is partial and jewelry-led. It supports scale, mood, and wearing logic rather than becoming
the main subject.

## Six Visual Modes

Choose one mode per poster. For a set, distribute complementary modes across independent
compositions.

### Editorial Presentation Board

- Usually vertical `3:4`.
- Combine one atmospheric model-wearing image, one close model detail, and one or two clean product
  cutouts on a refined color board.
- Use disciplined negative space, editorial hierarchy, controlled overlap, and premium magazine
  rhythm.
- Keep cutout edges clean, perspective natural, and contact shadows subtle.

### Luxury Ecommerce Homepage

- Often vertical `9:16` or a long homepage-like composition.
- Use an ivory or softly tinted panel with hero area, product cards, one model detail, one artistic
  scene, and minimal navigation or CTA when relevant.
- Favor quiet luxury, pale color, low saturation, natural light, and clear commercial hierarchy.
- Avoid fake checkout details, invented prices, random addresses, and noisy marketplace styling.

### Torn-Paper Campaign

- Usually vertical `3:4`.
- Use a matte paper ground, an upper torn opening with partial model wearing, a lower foreground tear,
  and jewelry that interacts coherently with the paper depth.
- Make fibers, irregular edges, and shadows tactile and premium rather than cartoon-like.
- Keep the worn jewelry anatomically and spatially coherent.

### Left-Right Split Poster

- Usually vertical `9:16`.
- Place a partial model-wearing crop on one side and a same-product cutout with a small text block on
  the other.
- Use modern editorial balance, strong negative space, and a clear reading order.
- The two product depictions must match exactly; keep the layout uncluttered.

### Japanese Magazine Catalog Page

- Usually vertical `3:4`.
- Use a white or warm-white page, one dominant hero product, several smaller product views, and airy
  numbering, captions, or category labels.
- Arrange elements freely with an intellectual, high-fashion editorial rhythm.
- Avoid rigid grids, equal-size marketplace tiles, and text that hides the jewelry.

### American Fashion Collage

- Usually vertical `3:4`.
- Choose collage elements from the jewelry’s own mood: paper scraps, small labels, line sketches,
  tape, stamps, outfit cutouts, plants, shells, architecture, ribbon, or notes.
- Use a relaxed but product-led hierarchy on white or warm white.
- Avoid fixed unrelated props, oversized headlines, and decorative overload.

## Variation Rules

- N requested posters means N independent compositions. Do not combine them into a contact sheet,
  grid, or long strip unless the user explicitly asks for a combined layout.
- Vary meaningful visual axes: crop, layout rhythm, background color, paper opening, product
  placement, typography style, time of light, model crop, product-card arrangement, or mood element.
- Do not vary product identity, category, stone count, metal color, supplied copy, or structural
  details.
- Every composition must make the jewelry the first visual signal and retain its own product-truth
  lock.

## Complex Campaign Visual Slots

Only for a user-requested multi-source or multi-slot campaign, assign distinct visual slots per
source design. A useful three-slot starting point is:

1. `PDP Hero / Luxury Ecommerce Homepage` — clear commercial hierarchy and primary product view.
2. `Editorial Board / Lookbook Presentation` — model context, cutout layering, and brand mood.
3. `Macro Detail / Magazine Catalog` — construction, stone setting, material, and editorial detail.

State the source design identity, slot role, template card, and visual difference inside each brief.
Do not import unrelated campaign fields. If the user asks for fewer or different slots, follow that
visual structure instead.

## Creative Brief Skeleton

```text
Create one high-quality [aspect ratio] [poster or ecommerce visual type].

Creative objective:
[one independent poster / ecommerce hero / PDP header / magazine page / collage poster]
for [supplied brand context or neutral jewelry context].

Template selection:
- Selected template card: [card from poster-template-knowledge-base.md]
- Template selection reason: [fit with intent, product, ratio, and allowed modules]
- Required layout modules: [at least two visible modules beyond the jewelry object]

Reference roles:
- Layout reference: [features to learn and identity not to copy]
- Jewelry truth reference: [reference that defines product facts]
- Mood reference: [palette, light, atmosphere, or texture only]
- Prior accepted render: [accepted identity to preserve, if present]

Product truth:
[category, silhouette, proportions, metal, stones, setting, structural counts, wearing structure,
and same-product requirement across all modules]

Composition:
[chosen mode; hero, model, cutout, product card, art scene, text, navigation, CTA, or label modules;
aspect ratio; negative space; hierarchy; crop]

Typography:
[exact supplied text or minimal-copy policy; placement, scale, contrast, and forbidden text]

Photography and material:
[jewelry reflections, skin texture when relevant, lighting, contact shadows, cutout quality,
background and panel materials]

Allowed visual changes:
[crop, light, small rotation, cleanup, panel color, layout rhythm, or mood variation]

Quality constraints:
- Product truth is more important than poster novelty.
- One independent composition only unless a combined layout was explicitly requested.
- Do not redesign the jewelry or add/remove product elements.
- anti-still-life-degeneration: retain the selected modules; do not reduce the composition to a
  centered product, soft background, shallow reflection, and one small title.
- Keep supplied text exact, typography controlled, and the jewelry unobscured.
- Do not invent claims, marks, prices, addresses, QR codes, watermarks, handles, or random text.
```
