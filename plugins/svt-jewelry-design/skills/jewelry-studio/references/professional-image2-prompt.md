# Professional Jewelry Creative Brief Reference

This is an on-demand creative reference, not a default dependency for every jewelry request. Read it
when the work needs a generation-ready visual brief, reference translation, several differentiated
designs, or complex jewelry, material, craft, composition, or manufacturability knowledge. A simple
request that already states the product and visual result does not need this whole reference.

Read `../../jewelry-design/references/design-presets.md` only when the requested material, craft,
or style needs its specialist cues. Do not load the complete preset library for an ordinary request.
This file supplies a creative-only brief body; execution, tools, providers, jobs, and delivery are
outside its scope.

## Creative Brief Core

Write a production art brief rather than a keyword pile. Include only details that determine the
product or visible result:

- objective and output type: product render, revision, reference sheet, grid cell, poster, catalog
  slot, ecommerce hero, or model-wearing visual
- product facts: jewelry category, count, scale, proportions, silhouette, focal hierarchy, design
  name or collection mood, intended wearer, and style identity
- materials: metal and color, finish, surface texture, enamel, pearl, gemstones, and mixed-material
  relationships
- gemstone plan: species or color direction, cut, transparency, relative scale, count, placement,
  hierarchy, and setting method
- wearable construction: prongs, bezel, halo, gallery, bridge, shank, bail, chain, clasp, hinge,
  post, cuff gap, articulation, support ribs, weight balance, and skin-facing comfort as relevant
- image direction: camera angle, crop, product scale, focal point, macro detail, depth of field,
  lighting, reflections, background, and shadow
- reference handling: the role of each reference, locked facts, and explicitly allowed changes
- visual constraints: only the negatives needed to prevent likely visible failure

## Reference Locking And Allowed Change

Classify each input before translating it. Never treat every reference as unrestricted inspiration.

- Loose stone: lock known species, color direction, cut, proportions, transparency, inclusions when
  identity-defining, and its role as the visual focus.
- Sketch or line art: lock the outer silhouette, proportions, symmetry or intended asymmetry,
  structural relationships, motif count, and composition center.
- Product or accepted render: lock category, outline, key geometry, stone count and positions,
  setting logic, wearing structure, and stated materials.
- Style or nature reference: lock only the named contour rhythm, layering, palette relationship,
  texture, movement, or ornament language; do not copy unrelated objects or scene details.

State allowed changes separately, such as category, metal support, secondary stones, setting system,
surface finish, craft texture, volume, or commercial polish. Repeat the small set of locked facts in
every revision brief so product identity does not drift.

## Design Identity And Series Differentiation

Translate a concept through category and scale, silhouette, focal plan, metal plan, gemstone plan,
construction, and craft texture. Give the piece a recognizable design identity rather than a list of
generic luxury adjectives.

For N requested designs, write N independently usable briefs. Keep the requested collection family,
but vary at least three meaningful axes across the set: category or wearing mode, silhouette, focal
geometry, negative space, stone hierarchy, motif treatment, setting rhythm, surface craft, or color
balance. Name the distinguishing idea of each design and avoid near-duplicates produced by changing
only a gem color or camera angle. Use a combined contact sheet only when explicitly requested.

Useful balance rules:

- Commercial high jewelry needs a clear visual center, gathered stone relationships, intentional
  fullness, and a complete silhouette rather than thin, scattered decoration.
- Delicate daily or Japanese-inspired jewelry needs petite proportions, light support, restrained
  stones, fine edges, and open breathing space rather than oversized luxury volume.
- Figurative, floral, ribbon, vine, geometric, or historical language should be visible in the
  silhouette and construction, not merely named in prose.
- Fusion should identify the dominant language and the secondary accent; do not stack incompatible
  craft modules indiscriminately.

## Materials, Gemstones, And Craft

Describe material behavior visibly and consistently:

- platinum and white gold have cool, clean reflections; yellow, rose, champagne, or high-purity gold
  must keep their distinct hue rather than collapsing into orange, gray, or generic chrome
- high polish produces controlled mirror highlights; satin, brushed, sandblasted, diamond-sand, and
  ancient-gold finishes need their own grain, highlight width, and edge behavior
- gemstones need plausible body color, transparency or opacity, cut geometry, fire, inclusions when
  relevant, and contact with a real setting; avoid glowing plastic or featureless colored glass
- enamel, pearl, carved stone, and mixed materials need specific surface depth and junctions with the
  metal rather than appearing painted onto it
- fine details such as filigree, milgrain, granulation, engraving, chasing, pave, rope borders, or
  openwork must have believable scale, attachment points, and hierarchy

When specialist treatment matters, load only the relevant section of `design-presets.md`, for example
filigree or chased gold, ancient gold, 3D hard gold, delicate fine jewelry, commercial high jewelry,
or Italian vintage high jewelry. Preserve its material contradictions and proportion warnings in the
brief; do not import unrelated preset sections.

## Manufacturability And Wearability

The visible design must explain how the jewelry exists and is worn:

- large or elevated stones need visible prongs, bezel walls, gallery, bridge, shoulder support, or
  ribs appropriate to the cut and scale
- pendants need a bail or connector with plausible orientation; necklaces need continuous chain and
  clasp logic; earrings need posts, hooks, clips, or closures
- bracelets and bangles need a hinge, opening, clasp, articulation, or cuff gap; moving parts need
  credible joints and clearance
- rings need a continuous wearable shank, sensible setting height, finger clearance, and balanced
  top weight; sharp edges and snag-prone unsupported projections should be resolved
- repeated motifs, pave fields, and openwork need minimum-looking metal bridges and attachment points;
  nothing should float, intersect impossibly, or rely on invisible support

When a concept intentionally tests a structural limit, keep the visual ambition but state the
support strategy. Do not hide unresolved construction behind dramatic lighting or a front-only view.

## Composition, Camera, Light, And Background

Describe the image as a real capture: view angle, orientation, crop, scale in frame, focal plane,
lens feeling, depth of field, reflection control, gem sparkle, edge clarity, contact shadow, and any
secondary detail view. Choose the view that proves the design and its wearing structure.

For an ordinary product/design image, the compact default is one complete jewelry item, centered in
a square `1:1` frame, on pure white or pale gray-white with soft studio light, controlled luxury
reflections, a crisp silhouette, and a subtle realistic contact shadow. This is a fallback visual
default, not a long template and not a rule for every output type.

Change scene or ratio when the request calls for a poster, ecommerce module, model wearing, catalog
lifestyle, campaign, dark or colored background, festive props, packaging, reference sheet, grid, or
another explicit composition. Make props support the product's scale and story without obscuring its
outline, stones, closures, or contact points.

## Necessary Visual Constraints

Select constraints that address the actual brief. Common safeguards include:

- no unrequested logo, watermark, brand, price, certificate, QR code, URL, social handle, or random
  text
- no extra jewelry pieces, gemstones, motifs, chains, connectors, fingers, or props beyond the stated
  composition
- no unsupported stones, floating metal, broken chain continuity, fused links, impossible closures,
  intersecting parts, or non-wearable openings
- no cheap plastic metal, muddy reflections, clipped highlights, glass-like opaque gems, rough mesh,
  soft edges, or accidental asymmetry
- no hand, model, stand, box, fabric, flowers, or scenery for the ordinary white-background product
  default unless requested

Do not fill the brief with universal negatives. Keep explicit text and branding constraints for
layouts that can contain typography, and keep product-fidelity constraints close to the facts they
protect.

## Poster And Ecommerce Visual Content

For a poster, campaign visual, ecommerce homepage, PDP hero, lookbook, magazine catalog page,
collage, product-card section, model-wearing image, or social cover, also read
`poster-ecommerce-image2-prompt.md` and the chosen template guidance only as needed.

Add these visual decisions:

- reference roles: layout reference, jewelry-truth reference, natural or mood reference, and prior
  accepted render; one reference may serve more than one role when stated
- product fidelity: category, outline, proportions, stone and motif counts, positions, metal and gem
  colors, setting logic, connectors, and wearing structure
- layout modules: choose a purposeful combination such as hero area, model detail, product cutout,
  product card, art scene, text block, navigation, CTA, label, numbering, or caption
- wearing mapping: scale, orientation, contact point, chain path, earring side, ring finger, bracelet
  opening, occlusion, and realistic skin contact
- typography: use exact supplied copy when present; otherwise keep text minimal and never invent
  brands, claims, prices, URLs, QR codes, watermarks, or social handles
- anti-degeneration: a requested poster or ecommerce layout must remain a composed layout with its
  required modules, not collapse into a centered white-background product still life

N requested posters means N independent poster briefs and images, not one N-up sheet unless the user
asks for a combined sheet.

## Creative-Only Brief Body

```text
Objective and output type:
[what visible result is needed, including count and format]

Product facts and design identity:
[category, name or concept, scale, silhouette, focal hierarchy, collection mood, wearer]

Locked reference facts:
[role of each reference and the geometry, counts, colors, materials, or structures that cannot drift]

Allowed changes:
[what may be reinterpreted or optimized]

Materials, gemstones, and craft:
[metal hue and finish, stone species/color/cut/count/placement, setting, texture, craft details]

Construction and wearability:
[support, gallery, shank/chain/bail/post/clasp/hinge/opening/articulation, balance, comfort]

Composition and camera:
[view, crop, scale, focal plane, macro/detail needs, depth of field]

Lighting and background:
[reflection behavior, gem light, background, shadow, scene and ratio when relevant]

Series differentiation or layout modules:
[the concept's distinguishing axes, or poster/ecommerce composition and wearing mapping]

Necessary visual constraints:
[only likely failure modes, text limits, fidelity locks, and structural negatives]
```
