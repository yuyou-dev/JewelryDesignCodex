# Jewelry Design Presets

Use these as compact defaults when the user gives a minimal brief. Treat them as design starting points, not mandatory templates.

## Ring

- Engagement / bridal: secure center setting, balanced shank, comfortable profile, refined shoulders.
- Cocktail / statement: larger center motif, stronger gallery, bolder shank, dramatic top view.
- Signet / band: metal form as the main design, engraving or inset stones, smooth wear comfort.
- Common ring details: prong count, bezel thickness, halo, hidden halo, gallery, bridge, cathedral shoulder, split shank, knife edge, pave, milgrain, engraving.
- High-jewelry ring: gathered center composition, full silhouette around the main stone, visible gallery support, orderly accent-stone rhythm, no loose floating stones.
- Japanese delicate ring: petite shank, lace-like shoulders, tiny accent diamonds, milgrain or beaded edge, restrained height and wearable profile.

## Necklace / Pendant

- Define length, chain style, pendant scale, bail or connector, clasp, and drape.
- Avoid floating pendants or chains too fragile for the focal weight.
- For a white-background style image, show the pendant and enough chain/clasp logic to prove wearability.
- For high-jewelry pendants, make the bail or connector part of the design instead of a generic loop.

## Earrings

- Define pair logic, attachment, closure, weight, movement, and on-ear direction.
- For asymmetry, describe left and right pieces separately.
- Studs need post/back logic; drops need hinge, jump ring, hook, or articulated connector.
- Keep delicate earrings light enough visually; heavy stone clusters need visible structural support.

## Bracelet

- Define structure, closure, hinge, cuff gap, articulation, repeated stones, and wrist comfort.
- Avoid rigid shapes without an opening or hinge.
- Bangles need hinge, clasp, or cuff gap unless they are explicitly slip-on.
- Tennis and station bracelets need repeated-link rhythm, stone security, and flexible articulation.

## Material Defaults

- Platinum: cool white, high polish, durable luxury bridal/high-jewelry signal.
- 18k white gold: bright white luxury, often rhodium-like in visuals.
- 18k yellow gold: warm classic, strong contrast with sapphire, emerald, ruby, pearl.
- Diamond accents: use as light control; keep them subordinate unless the brief asks for a diamond-forward piece.
- Sapphire: specify royal blue, cornflower blue, or deep ink blue only as design direction unless the user provides grade or origin.
- 24k / ancient gold look: rich warm yellow gold, satin matte or soft diffuse finish, fine grain, rounded handcrafted edges; avoid orange/copper, gray, pale 18k, mirror polish, oxidation, or dirty bronze patina.
- 3D hard gold: electroformed, rounded, chubby, inflated volume with matte sandblasted surface and subtle micro glitter; optional enamel must remain a small accent.
- Italian brushed gold: 18k yellow, white, or two-tone gold with fine parallel brushing, soft satin sheen, rope borders, bead edges, carved bezels, and openwork metal lace.
- Champagne / light gold: useful for delicate Japanese or light-luxury pieces; keep it refined and small-scale rather than heavy yellow-gold craft.

## Gem And Setting Defaults

- Main-stone hierarchy: name the focal stone first, then accent stones; state color, cut, transparency, placement, and relative scale.
- Commercial high jewelry: use orderly combinations of pear, marquise, oval, emerald, cushion, round, baguette, trapezoid, princess, and tapered cuts to create rhythm around the center stone.
- Delicate jewelry: tiny round diamonds, seed pearls, small colored stones, and micro pave should read like fine highlights, not a heavy surface.
- Setting vocabulary: prong, bezel, halo, hidden halo, pave, micro pave, channel, bead setting, milgrain, rope border, carved bezel, gallery, bridge, cathedral shoulder.
- Large stones need visible support: prongs, bezel wall, gallery, under-bezel, bridge, or side ribs. Avoid unsupported floating stones.

## Craft Modules

- Filigree gold: fine gold wires, scrolls, floral motifs, lace-like openwork, layered depth, tiny gold beads, crossing nodes. Full filigree covers most of the visible surface; mixed filigree uses small openwork zones against solid gold.
- Chased gold: bas-relief rising from metal, soft compressed hammered edges, layered depth, dense micro lines, warm raised highlights, deeper gold recessed shadows.
- Ancient gold: calm matte gold skin, fine grain, soft diffuse reflection, non-mirror warmth, rounded handmade edges.
- 3D hard gold: soft embossed details rather than sharp carving, hollow-lightweight feel, big rounded edges, commercial clean product finish.
- Japanese antique fine jewelry: petite, airy, floral/leaf/vine/lace/crown language, milgrain, beaded edges, tiny diamonds like dew.
- Light luxury daily jewelry: simple wearable 14k/18k gold lines, abstract bow/ribbon/petal/leaf/moon/star/fruit/waterdrop motifs, sparse stones, polished finish.
- Commercial high jewelry: glamorous but clean, gathered and full, centered on one or two main stones, with orderly diamond rhythm and minimal figurative symbolism.
- Italian vintage high jewelry: Renaissance/court ornament, brushed satin gold, rope or bead borders, carved bezels, metal lace, scrolls, fan shapes, arches, radiating crowns.

## White Product Image Defaults

- Default for ordinary style/design prompts: one centered jewelry item on a pure white or very pale gray-white opaque background.
- Include soft studio lighting, crisp silhouette, realistic metal reflection, gem fire, and a subtle contact shadow.
- Exclude hands, models, boxes, text, logos, watermarks, price tags, certificates, colored backgrounds, and props unless the user asks for them.
